Flood risk / GeoAIKuala Lumpur, Malaysia

Predicting where flood risk is most likely to concentrate.

Challenge
Accurate flood models are often black boxes. Planners need to know why a district is flagged before they commit drainage budgets or restrict development.
Approach
Twelve terrain, hydrological, rainfall and land-cover layers were assembled against historical flood records. Three models were benchmarked, then opened up with Accumulated Local Effects to expose the thresholds at which each factor raises or lowers risk.
Technology
Python, XGBoost, random forest, logistic regression, ALE, GIS terrain and hydrology processing.
Outcome
City-wide susceptibility maps validated against recorded floods, with rainfall, river proximity, elevation, slope and vegetation identified as the dominant drivers and explicit thresholds for each.
0.98AUC, ensemble models
94%Classification accuracy
900Flood and non-flood reference points

Urban change / Earth observationKuala Lumpur, 2000 to 2020

Twenty years of urban expansion, revealed from space.

Challenge
Cities change faster than hazard maps are updated. Showing how growth has shifted flood risk requires a consistent land-cover record across two decades.
Approach
Satellite imagery from 2000 and 2020 was classified into land-cover maps at 30 m resolution, change was quantified between them, and explainable machine learning linked that change statistically to shifting flood risk.
Technology
Google Earth Engine, Landsat archive, land-cover classification, NDVI and NDBI indices, change detection, explainable ML.
Outcome
A measured account of where the city changed. An already dense city kept filling in: a quarter of its remaining vegetation was lost, most of it to built-up land.

Landfill suitability, Lagos State · five classesTella et al. 2024 · CC BY 4.0

Site suitability / Decision supportLagos State, Nigeria

Finding where a megacity can safely site new infrastructure.

Challenge
Rapid urbanisation has absorbed Lagos's existing landfills. New sites must balance water bodies, settlements, roads, geology and land use, and the choice has to be defensible.
Approach
Nine spatial criteria were weighted with a fuzzy analytic hierarchy process informed by experts and residents, then combined in GIS into a state-wide suitability surface.
Technology
GIS-based multi-criteria decision making, fuzzy AHP, remote sensing, terrain and proximity analysis.
Outcome
A ranked map covering the whole state, showing that high-suitability land is scarce and concentrated in Epe, Ikorodu and Ibeju-Lekki.

Environmental intelligenceSelangor, Malaysia

Turning sparse air-quality observations into continuous spatial intelligence.

Challenge
Monitoring stations are few and far apart, and pollution shifts with weather and season. Agencies need to know what is happening between stations, and what drives it.
Approach
Station records were combined with climatic and land variables. Machine-learning, geostatistical and regression models were compared for predicting PM10 across the state and locating persistent hotspots.
Technology
Random forest, XGBoost, K-nearest neighbours, support vector regression, kriging, IDW, GIS.
Outcome
State-wide PM10 prediction and hotspot maps, with the influence of climate and season quantified, across four peer-reviewed studies.

Published in Environmental Science and Pollution Research (2021) and Atmospheric Pollution Research (2021). The visual is an illustrative rendering.

How we are different

Models you can trust beyond the training area.

A model learns from the places where data happened to exist, then gets applied everywhere else. In unfamiliar terrain it can be badly wrong and look exactly as confident.

We map the model's area of applicability: the part of your region where conditions resemble what the model has seen, and predictions can be relied on. Outside it, the map says so.

We do not just ask whether a model is accurate. We ask where it remains reliable.
